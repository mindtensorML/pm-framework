Portfolio Construction Framework

From purpose and policy to construction, execution and feedback, routed by objective type and by method for each sleeve.

v2.2, October 2026Mapped against the CFA Institute portfolio management process and IPS elementsA general method with no client data

The map follows CFA Institute's planning, execution and feedback process in seven steps. A gate in Step 2 routes each portfolio by objective type. A gate in Step 4 routes each sleeve by method, and illiquid deals run through their own subprocess. Feedback in Step 7 sends work back to execution, to the method or to the mandate. Each task cites a public standard where one speaks to it. Where none does, the task is marked as practitioner practice.

Who signs

Every task and gate has a named owner role. The investment committee or the client approves the IPS and the target portfolio, and the board approves the mandate. Commitments to counterparties, judgments on managers and brokers, and every sign-off stay with people. Where a model drafts, the owner reviews and signs. The tags are design estimates of what a frontier model could do with the owner accountable. They are not measured results.

  • Human-onlyCommitments, counterparty and people judgments, and sign-offs5
  • HybridA model drafts, the owner reviews and signs41
  • Model-runA model executes, the owner is accountable and samples the output12
  • Decision gateA decision with a named owner10
  • External parameterRead from law, governing documents or the actuary, never chosen by the portfolio team3
Context

Where the steps sit in the portfolio management process

CFA Institute describes portfolio management as planning, then execution, then feedback that loops back to both. The seven steps below sit inside those three stages. References use short names, listed in full under Sources. 5 nodes rest on practitioner practice alone, and 19 more draw on it in part.

Step 01

Define Purpose

What good looks likeThe investor, the assets in scope and the governing body are named first. The IPS later records who sets policy, who carries it out and who monitors it. The governing board approves the mandate before any rules are written.
Standard anchors · IPS s1a, s1b, s2a to s2d, s3a; PMP s5.1.2; PAA goals-based sub-portfolios
  1. PU-01HybridChief investment officer

    Identify the investor and the assets in scope

    Name the legal entity and the pools of assets this mandate governs. A model drafts the profile from the governing documents.

    IPS s1a
  2. PU-02HybridChief investment officer

    Identify beneficiaries and their order of priority

    Who the assets serve and whose claim comes first when objectives conflict.

    PMP s5.1.2; IPS s3e example on the exclusive benefit of beneficiaries; Practitioner practice for the priority order
  3. PU-03HybridGoverning board

    Define the capital objectives

    Relate the purpose of the assets to one or more broad objectives. For a strategic holding this is the reason to hold and the triggers that would end it.

    IPS s3a
  4. PU-04HybridGoverning board

    Set governance and delegation

    Who decides, who recommends, who executes, who monitors and reports, who escalates to whom, and who hires and dismisses external managers. Powers the board keeps for itself are listed apart from those it delegates. Mandates where the owner also decides need written limits on behavioral risk, such as a cooling-off rule for unplanned changes.

    IPS s1b, s2a, s2c, s2d, s3b example
  5. PU-05HybridChief investment officer

    Choose the portfolio architecture

    Parallel objectives get separate sub-portfolios. Compound objectives stay in one pool with every filter applied to it.

    PAA summary on goals-based sub-portfolios; Practitioner practice
  6. PU-06HybridChief investment officer

    Draft the mandate statement

    One document that brings the investor, beneficiaries, objectives, governance and architecture together.

    IPS s1a, s1b
  7. PU-D1Decision gateGoverning board

    Approve the mandate?

    The board approves the mandate or sends it back with reasons. A revision starts again at PU-01, and steps the board did not question are confirmed rather than redrafted.

    IPS s1b, s2a
2
Step 02

Define Rules in the Investment Policy Statement

What good looks likeThe IPS states return and risk objectives and five constraints. These are liquidity, time horizon, tax, legal and regulatory limits, and unique circumstances. It also sets benchmarks, the ESG and stewardship policy and the rebalancing rules. The investment committee or the client approves it.
Standard anchors · IPS s2b, s2f, s3a to s3e, s4c; PMP s5.1.1, s5.1.2; GS-BM; GIPS-AO 21.A.15; UKSC 2026; TPR LDI
  1. RU-D1Decision gateInvestment committee

    Which objective type?

    Five routes set what the rules must cover. A is return-seeking against a benchmark, B is absolute return, C is liability-driven, D has multiple objectives and splits the mandate into sub-portfolios, and E is a strategic holding. Under D the gate is asked again for each sub-portfolio, which then takes route A, B, C or E.

    PMP s5.1.1; PAA introduction
  2. RU-10HybridChief investment officer

    List the sub-portfolios and their objective types

    Route D only. Each sub-portfolio from the PU-05 architecture is named with its objective type and the assets it holds. Each one then passes through the objective gate in turn.

    PAA summary on goals-based sub-portfolios; Practitioner practice
  3. RU-P1External parameterActuary

    Liability profile

    Read from the latest actuarial valuation. Cash flows, duration, inflation linkage and the funding position. The portfolio team does not choose them.

    PAA summary on liability characteristics; TPR LDI investment strategy
  4. RU-01HybridChief investment officer

    Set the return objective

    The required return and how it relates to spending, inflation or liabilities. Route B states a target return over a stated period.

    IPS s3b; PMP s6.1.2
  5. RU-02HybridChief investment officer

    Set risk tolerance and the risk budget

    Ability and willingness to bear risk, the largest tolerable loss, and the absolute and active risk budgets. The risk budget is approved with the IPS at RU-D2, or by the board where the IPS reserves it. Route E sets downside limits only. If the required return needs more risk than the investor can bear, the CIO puts the conflict to the committee rather than raising risk to fit.

    IPS s3c, s2f; PMP s6.1.1, s6.1.2; PAA learning outcomes on risk budgets
  6. RU-03HybridChief investment officer

    Set the time horizon and evaluation horizon

    The investment horizon, any stages within it, and the minimum period before underperformance triggers action. Illiquid sleeves get a longer evaluation period.

    IPS s3d; PMP s5.1.1, s6.2.2
  7. RU-04HybridChief investment officer

    Set liquidity, tax, leverage and currency limits

    Cash needs and the liquid floor, tax treatment, leverage and derivative limits, foreign asset limits and the currency hedging policy.

    IPS s3d; PMP s5.1.1, s6.2.1, s6.2.3
  8. RU-P2External parameterCompliance officer

    Legal and regulatory limits

    Read from law, the regulator and the governing documents. Eligible assets, concentration caps and prudence rules. Compliance confirms them and the portfolio team does not set them.

    PMP s5.1.1, s6.2.4; IPS s3d
  9. RU-05HybridChief investment officer

    Record unique circumstances and advantages

    Restrictions particular to this investor and any lasting advantages it holds, such as a long horizon, a sponsor's support or access to private deals.

    PMP s5.1.1, s6.2.5; Practitioner practice for advantages
  10. RU-06HybridChief investment officer

    Set the ESG, exclusion and stewardship policy

    Whether ESG factors are used and how. Exclusions, proxy voting authority, engagement and escalation, and any securities lending limits.

    IPS s3e; UKSC 2026; Practitioner practice for escalation
  11. RU-07HybridChief investment officer

    Select benchmarks

    A policy benchmark for the total fund and one per asset class. Each is specified in advance, relevant, measurable, investable and complete where possible. Route B uses a target return. Route E records that no benchmark is appropriate and why. The total fund policy benchmark still carries the holding, at its actual or target return.

    IPS s3b; GS-BM valid benchmark characteristics; GIPS-AO 21.A.15
  12. RU-08HybridChief investment officer

    Set the rebalancing policy and review cycle

    Ranges around each target and calendar or threshold triggers. The policy names who acts on a breach, by when, how far back toward target and how the trades are funded. Illiquid sleeves get wider ranges, since their trading costs are high and under-allocation may persist while commitments are drawn. It also sets how often the IPS itself is reviewed.

    IPS s4c and example, s3b example, s2b; PAA summary on corridor width; Vanguard 2015
  13. RU-09HybridChief investment officer

    Draft the investment policy statement

    A model drafts the IPS from the approved inputs. The CIO checks it against the mandate and every constraint. Under Route D the IPS carries one section per sub-portfolio and is approved as one document.

    IPS all sections; PMP s5.1.2
  14. RU-D2Decision gateInvestment committee or client

    Approve the IPS?

    The committee or the client approves the IPS, or sends it back with the points to change. A revision returns to the objective gate, so each route re-enters at its own first step.

    IPS s2a, s1b

Step 03

Map the Opportunity Set

What good looks likeThe eligible universe follows from the approved IPS. Every asset class on the list needs a benchmark, a valuation method and a working route for custody and settlement before money goes in.
Standard anchors · IPS s3b, s3d; GIPS-F 2.A.19 to 2.A.21 valuation; Practitioner practice
  1. OP-01HybridChief investment officer

    List eligible asset classes

    Every permissible asset class with a short description and its benchmark, including classes not used today.

    IPS s3b
  2. OP-02HybridPortfolio manager

    List instruments, vehicles and deal types

    Securities, funds, separate accounts, co-investments and derivatives, within the leverage and securities lending limits.

    IPS s3d, s3e
  3. OP-03Model-runPortfolio manager

    List markets, geographies and sourcing channels

    A model compiles the market list against the foreign investment and currency limits. The owner samples it.

    IPS s3d
  4. OP-04HybridPortfolio manager

    List eligible strategies

    Passive, active and enhanced index approaches by sleeve. New asset types that span several classes are filed by their main economic function, with cross-references.

    PMP s5.1.2
  5. OP-05HybridOperations lead

    Check operational readiness

    Custody, pricing and valuation source, legal eligibility and settlement route for each new asset type. Digital assets need a qualified custodian and a tested way to settle.

    Practitioner practice; GIPS-F 2.A.19 to 2.A.21 on fair value
  6. OP-06HybridChief investment officer

    Formalize the eligible universe

    The approved list. The IPS names every permissible asset class, so a class it does not already permit needs an IPS change approved by the committee before it is added. Under Route D each sub-portfolio leaves here on its own route.

    IPS s3b, s2a
4
Step 04

Choose the Selection or Allocation Methodology

What good looks likeThe strategic allocation combines the IPS with capital market expectations. Recognized methods include constrained mean-variance, risk budgeting and risk parity, factor-based, liability-relative and goals-based allocation. Each sleeve then follows a discretionary, systematic or liability-matching route.
Standard anchors · PMP s5.1.3, s5.1.4, s5.2; PAA introduction and summary; IPS s2e; TPR LDI; PRA SS1/23 Principle 4 by analogy
  1. ME-01HybridChief investment officer

    Form capital market expectations

    Long-run return, volatility and correlation assumptions for each asset class. A model drafts them from stated methods and data. The CIO signs the assumptions and records who set them.

    PMP s5.1.3; IPS s2e
  2. ME-02HybridChief investment officer

    Choose the allocation framework

    Constrained mean-variance, Black-Litterman, risk budgeting or risk parity, factor-based, surplus or hedging plus return-seeking, or goals-based sub-portfolios. The choice is justified against the objective type.

    PAA introduction and summary
  3. ME-03HybridChief investment officer

    Set tactical and dynamic adjustment rules

    How far and for how long the portfolio may depart from the strategic weights, within the IPS ranges.

    PMP s5.2
  4. ME-D1Decision gateChief investment officer

    Which method for this sleeve?

    Each sleeve takes one route. Mixed funds run several sleeves on different routes.

    PAA introduction; Practitioner practice
  5. ME-04HybridPortfolio manager

    Set the selection criteria

    Research and selection rules for discretionary sleeves, including manager selection where external managers are used. Illiquid deals go through the deal subprocess.

    PMP s5.1.2; IPS s2c
  6. ME-05HybridPortfolio manager

    Specify and build the systematic model

    Rules, data, out-of-sample tests and capacity. A model drafts code and tests to the specification, and the portfolio manager signs the build.

    Practitioner practice
  7. ME-D2Decision gateModel risk lead

    Is the model validated for live use?

    The model risk lead is independent of the builder. The validator reruns the tests, challenges the data and the assumptions, and passes the model, sends it back, or rejects it.

    PRA SS1/23 Principle 4.1, 4.2 by analogy; Practitioner practice
  8. ME-P1External parameterActuary

    Liability profile for the hedged liabilities

    Read from the latest signed actuarial valuation, the same way as RU-P1. Under Route C it is the RU-P1 profile, confirmed as current. Outside Route C it is read here for the liabilities the sleeve hedges. The portfolio team does not build it.

    PAA summary on liability characteristics; TPR LDI investment strategy
  9. ME-06HybridChief investment officer

    Design the liability hedge

    Hedge ratios for rates and inflation, the collateral buffer and the order in which assets meet cash calls. For UK pension schemes, TPR guidance asks leveraged hedges to hold a market stress buffer of at least 250 bps on top of an operational buffer. Route C mandates use this route for their hedging sleeve.

    PAA summary on hedging and return-seeking portfolios; TPR LDI collateral resilience
  10. ME-07HybridChief investment officer

    Formalize the allocation model

    The framework, inputs, sleeve routes and adjustment rules in one document for the committee.

    IPS s2e

Step 05

Construct the Portfolio

What good looks likeConstruction turns the allocation model into target weights inside the IPS limits. Limits belong inside the optimizer, not after it. The investment committee approves the strategic allocation and the target portfolio before money moves.
Standard anchors · PMP s5.1.4, s5.2; PAA summary; IPS s2e, s3b; TPR LDI
  1. CO-01HybridRisk manager

    Load IPS limits and operational limits into the optimizer

    Weight ranges, risk budget, liquidity floor, leverage and counterparty limits. The risk manager checks every limit against the IPS.

    PMP s5.1.4; PAA summary on weight constraints
  2. CO-02Model-runPortfolio manager

    Optimize risk and return

    A model runs the chosen framework with the limits in place as hard constraints. Every weight a limit cuts is logged with the limit that cut it. The portfolio manager owns the run and samples the inputs and outputs.

    PAA summary on weight constraints; PMP s5.2; Practitioner practice for the log
  3. CO-03HybridPortfolio manager

    Set target weights, ranges and the pacing plan

    Targets and ranges per sleeve. Liquid sleeves can move in one step. Illiquid sleeves follow a commitment pacing plan through commitment, drawdown, holding and exit, with liquid assets held against unfunded commitments. Systematic sleeves adjust continuously.

    IPS s3b; PAA learning outcome on asset class liquidity; Practitioner practice for commitment pacing
  4. CO-04HybridRisk manager

    Stress test and run scenarios

    Historic and hypothetical shocks, liquidity under stress, and collateral calls on any leveraged hedge. A model runs the shocks and proposes scenarios. The risk manager chooses the hypothetical scenarios and signs the results.

    PAA learning outcomes on Monte Carlo and scenarios; TPR LDI
  5. CO-05Model-runHead of trading

    Estimate implementation and transaction costs

    Commissions, spreads, market impact, taxes and the cost of trades that cannot be filled, set against the expected benefit of the move.

    PMP s5.2; TMG definition of best execution; PAA summary on trading costs
  6. CO-06HybridPortfolio manager

    Finalize the target portfolio

    The target, the ranges, the stress results and the cost estimate in one paper for the committee.

    IPS s3b
  7. CO-D1Decision gateInvestment committee

    Approve the strategic allocation and target portfolio?

    The committee approves, sends it back, or finds that the target needs a change to the IPS. Where the IPS reserves the policy portfolio to the board, the committee recommends and the board approves.

    IPS s2e, s3b

Step 06

Execute

What good looks likeBest execution means taking all sufficient steps to get the best possible result. Price, costs, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, size and nature all count. CFA Institute's guidelines judge it over many trades, through process, disclosure and records.
Standard anchors · MiFID II Art 27(1); ESMA 2024; TMG 1.A to 1.D, 2, 3; PMP s5.2
  1. EX-01HybridHead of trading

    Plan the execution strategy

    Order of trades, timing, pace, transition plan and the route for each asset type. Route E holdings take no action unless a trigger set at PU-03 is hit, and a hit trigger goes to the board before any trade is planned.

    TMG definition of best execution; TMG 1.A; PMP s5.2
  2. EX-D1Decision gateGoverning board

    Act on the Route E trigger?

    A trigger set at PU-03 has been hit. The investment committee reviews it and recommends, and the board decides, since it set the trigger. The holding can be kept with the reason minuted, sold or reduced, or sent back for a review of the mandate. A decision to sell or reduce returns to EX-01, which plans the sale.

    IPS s1b, s2a; Practitioner practice
  3. EX-02Model-runCompliance officer

    Run pre-trade compliance checks

    Rule checks against IPS limits, restricted lists, concentration and counterparty limits before any order leaves. Only the compliance officer can clear a breach. A breach on a deal approved at DP-D2 goes back to negotiation, so changed terms return to the committee.

    Practitioner practice; IPS s1b; TMG 1.A.6
  4. EX-03Human-onlyHead of trading

    Select brokers, venues and counterparties

    Choose from the approved list by execution quality, financial strength and settlement record. Judging a counterparty stays with people.

    TMG 1.B, 1.C; MiFID II Art 27(1)
  5. EX-04HybridHead of trading

    Work liquid orders

    The trader releases each order. Execution algorithms work it inside the trader's limits, and the trader watches fills and market impact.

    MiFID II Art 27(1); TMG 1.A
  6. EX-05Human-onlyAuthorized signatories

    Sign commitments and manager appointments

    Sign subscription or purchase documents and the investment management agreement for any newly appointed external manager, after investment and operational due diligence. The integrity, sanctions, anti-money-laundering and conflict checks on the counterparty or manager must be on file and current. Fund capital calls and record unfunded commitments. Only authorized signatories commit the portfolio.

    IPS s1b, s2a, s2c; FATF R.6, R.7 for sanctions; Practitioner practice for integrity checks
  7. EX-06Model-runOperations lead

    Confirm, settle and reconcile

    Match confirmations, settle, reconcile with the custodian and check that fills were allocated fairly across accounts.

    TMG 1.D, 3; Practitioner practice for settlement and reconciliation
  8. EX-07Model-runOperations lead

    Record the live portfolio

    Positions, cost and exposures as booked. This is the start point for monitoring.

    IPS s2f; Practitioner practice
  9. EX-08Model-runHead of trading

    Analyze transaction costs after the trade

    Compare costs with the pre-trade estimate and with benchmarks by broker, venue and method. Results feed broker reviews. The 2024 MiFID II review deletes the annual top five venue report, but the duty to monitor execution quality remains.

    TMG 1.A.5, 1.C; MiFID II Art 27(7); ESMA 2024; PMP s5.2

Step 07

Monitor and Adapt

What good looks likeFeedback has two parts. Monitoring and rebalancing keep the portfolio inside the IPS. Performance evaluation measures, attributes and appraises results. Returns follow GIPS, with time-weighted returns for the total fund.
Standard anchors · PMP s5.3.1, s5.3.2; IPS s2b, s2c, s2f, s4a to s4c; GIPS-AO 21.A.25, 21.A.26; GIPS-F 1.A.35; UKSC 2026; Vanguard 2015
  1. MO-08Model-runOperations lead

    Check position, price and cash data

    Before any return, risk or drift figure, a model checks the booked positions, prices and cash for stale, missing, duplicated or unreconciled items, including lagged private fund valuations. Each flag names the figures it would distort. A blocking flag stops those figures until operations fixes it.

    IPS s2f example on independent custodian accounting; Practitioner practice
  2. MO-01Model-runPerformance analyst

    Measure performance

    Time-weighted returns for the total fund, which GIPS requires of asset owners. Money-weighted returns may be added and suit private market sleeves. A firm reporting to clients may show money-weighted returns only if it controls the cash flows and the strategy is closed-end, fixed life, fixed commitment or mainly illiquid. Gross and net of fees against each benchmark.

    GIPS-AO 21.A.25, 21.A.26; GIPS-F 1.A.35
  3. MO-02Model-runRisk manager

    Monitor risk, limits and compliance

    Risk against the budget, tracking error, liquidity, leverage and every IPS limit. A breach goes to the risk manager and the compliance officer the same day, and a breach that needs trades goes straight to execution. When a delegated cure is booked, the risk manager confirms the position is back inside every limit and range, and the cure is reported in the current cycle rather than opening a new one.

    IPS s2f, s4b
  4. MO-03Model-runPerformance analyst

    Attribute performance

    Allocation, selection and timing effects for the total fund and each manager, and factor exposures where a risk model is used.

    PMP s5.3.2; IPS s4b
  5. MO-04Human-onlyChief investment officer

    Review external managers and stewardship outcomes

    Whether each manager still fits its mandate, and what voting and engagement achieved. A model compiles the data. The judgment on each manager stays with people.

    IPS s2c; UKSC 2026
  6. MO-05HybridChief investment officer

    Reassess investor circumstances, markets and benchmarks

    Changes in the investor's needs, in capital market expectations, and in whether each benchmark is still a fair measure. An IPS past its scheduled review date is flagged to the committee.

    PMP s5.3.1, s5.3.2; IPS s2b
  7. MO-06Model-runPortfolio manager

    Check drift against the rebalancing ranges

    Compare weights with targets and ranges on the calendar or threshold set in the IPS. A liquid sleeve outside its range goes straight to execution under the CIO's delegated authority, and the committee sees it in the next report. An illiquid sleeve outside its range is a signal to adjust commitment pacing, not a trade, and it is reported the same way.

    IPS s4c, s3b example; Vanguard 2015; PAA summary on corridors
  8. MO-07HybridChief investment officer

    Report to the investment committee and stakeholders

    Returns, attribution, risk, breaches, drift, manager and stewardship reviews and a recommended action. A model drafts it and the CIO signs.

    IPS s4a, s2b
  9. MO-D1Decision gateInvestment committee

    What adjustment is needed?

    Decided on the report and minuted. Range breaches were already rebalanced under delegation. The committee can still order a rebalance within policy, change the strategy or method, or send the mandate to the board. A change to a Route E holding goes to the board, since its triggers were set at PU-03.

    PMP s5.3.1; IPS s2b, s4c
SUB
Subprocess

Illiquid Deal Process

What good looks likePrivate deals for discretionary sleeves. The committee releases due diligence spend at one gate and approves negotiated terms at a second. Valuation follows current fair value guidance.
Standard anchors · IPEV 2025; IPS s2a; FATF R.6, R.7; OECD PFG Guideline 9; Practitioner practice
  1. DP-01HybridDeal team lead

    Source opportunities

    A model scans the pipeline against the selection criteria. The deal lead decides which to pursue.

    Practitioner practice
  2. DP-02HybridDeal team lead

    Screen against the mandate

    Eligibility, exclusions, size, sector and concentration. A model drafts the screening note.

    IPS s3b, s3e; Practitioner practice
  3. DP-07Human-onlyCompliance officer

    Run integrity, sanctions, anti-money-laundering and conflict checks

    Checks on the sponsor, the counterparties and their owners against sanctions lists, adverse media and anti-money-laundering rules, plus any conflict with the firm or a committee member. A model can run the list screening. The judgment on each party stays with the compliance officer, who records every conflicted member so that member is recused at DP-D1 and DP-D2. A confirmed sanctions match ends the deal, since no committee can approve funds for a designated party.

    FATF R.6, R.7 for sanctions; OECD PFG Guideline 9 annotation for conflicts and recusal; Practitioner practice for integrity and anti-money-laundering checks
  4. DP-03HybridDeal team lead

    Preliminary due diligence

    Key risks, a first view of value and the cost of full due diligence.

    Practitioner practice
  5. DP-D1Decision gateInvestment committee

    Proceed to detailed due diligence?

    Release the due diligence budget, ask for more work, or decline. A red flag from DP-07 comes with a recommendation from the compliance officer, and recused members take no part.

    IPS s2a; OECD PFG Guideline 9 annotation on recusal; Practitioner practice
  6. DP-04HybridDeal team lead

    Detailed due diligence

    Commercial, financial, legal, tax, ESG and operational work with external advisers. A model drafts summaries, and each adviser signs its own report.

    Practitioner practice
  7. DP-05HybridValuation lead

    Value the investment

    Fair value and the price range, using methods consistent with the valuation guidelines.

    IPEV 2025
  8. DP-06Human-onlyDeal team lead

    Negotiate terms

    Price, structure, governance rights and conditions. Negotiation with a counterparty stays with people.

    Practitioner practice
  9. DP-D2Decision gateInvestment committee

    Approve the investment on negotiated terms?

    Final approval on the negotiated terms, a request to rework them, or a decline. Recused members take no part. Approval also confirms the deal fits the commitment pacing plan. Where CO-D1 has not yet approved a plan, the commitment waits for it.

    IPS s2a; OECD PFG Guideline 9 annotation on recusal

Open questions for practitioners

The map follows the standards closely, but some choices rest on judgment. These are the ones where an experienced CIO can improve it most.

  1. Should routine rebalancing inside the ranges go straight to trading?

    A sleeve outside its range goes from MO-06 to EX-01 under the CIO's delegated authority, and the committee sees it afterwards in MO-07. Some committees want to approve every rebalance first. Which is closer to practice for a multi-asset fund?

  2. Where should the illiquid deal process sit?

    It branches from the selection criteria in Step 4 and returns at pre-trade checks in Step 6. Pacing is set in CO-03. Would a CIO rather see deals sourced against the pacing plan after construction?

  3. Should the liability-matching method route stay separate from the liability-driven objective route?

    Route C sets the objective and Route Z builds the hedge. Merging them would simplify the map but lose hedging sleeves inside return-seeking mandates.

  4. Which tasks should a model never draft, even with a person signing?

    The current split is 5 human-only, 41 model drafts and 12 model-run. Capital market expectations and the risk budget are tagged as drafts the owner signs.
